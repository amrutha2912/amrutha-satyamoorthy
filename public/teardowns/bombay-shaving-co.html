Bombay Shaving Company: The premium brand that went mass and lost its moat

What happens when a D2C brand with a clear positioning decides to compete with Gillette on their terms, and why SKU proliferation is the quiet killer of brand identity.
Men's Grooming Premium → Mass Pivot D2C + Retail Expansion
Brand Snapshot

India's Dollar Shave Club moment, and the pivot that diluted it

Founded in 2016 by Shantanu Deshpande, Bombay Shaving Company (BSC) launched with a compelling thesis: Indian men deserve a better shaving experience than mass-market disposables, and they'll pay a premium for it. The early brand was sharp, razors, shaving cream, aftershave. Clean design. D2C subscription model. A clear alternative to Gillette for the urban, image-conscious Indian man.

It worked. BSC raised over ₹200Cr in funding, built a strong D2C customer base, and became the reference point for Indian D2C men's grooming. Then came the pivot: BSC expanded into body wash, face wash, beard care, hair removal cream, deodorants, and skincare. The product line ballooned from ~10 SKUs to 50+. Distribution shifted from D2C to mass retail.

This raises a big question: the oldest one in brand strategy: can you go mass without losing the premium? BSC's trajectory suggests the answer is complicated.

Strategic Evolution

The three eras of BSC

2016–2019: The Razor Era

Clear positioning: premium shaving. D2C subscription model. 10–12 SKUs, all shaving-adjacent. Brand identity was tight, you knew exactly what BSC was. Comparable to Dollar Shave Club or Harry's in the US. Strong founder narrative (IIM-L grad challenging Gillette).

2019–2022: The Expansion Era

SKU explosion into face care, body care, hair removal, beard grooming. Raised ₹45Cr Series A, then ₹160Cr. Entered offline retail aggressively. Positioning shifted from "premium shaving" to "men's grooming." The category got broader. The brand got blurrier.

2022–Present: The Identity Crisis Era

Competing simultaneously against Gillette (mass razors), Beardo (beard/grooming), Man Matters (wellness), and Nivea Men (body care). No single category owned. The subscription model that defined early BSC is no longer the primary growth driver. Offline retail and marketplace sales dominate.

Strategic Analysis

Porter's Five Forces: why the mass pivot makes BSC vulnerable

HIGH

Competitive Rivalry

BSC now competes in 5+ subcategories simultaneously. In razors: Gillette (80%+ market share). In beard care: Beardo, Ustraa. In skincare: Man Matters, Nivea Men, Garnier. In body care: every FMCG brand. They face a dominant incumbent in every category they've entered.

HIGH

Threat of Substitutes

Every BSC product except razors has dozens of substitutes at lower price points. A ₹200 BSC face wash competes with ₹99 Nivea and ₹149 Garnier. The switching cost is zero, men's grooming products have almost no lock-in effect.

MED

Buyer Power

Men's grooming is a low-involvement category for most Indian men. Brand loyalty is weak. Purchase decisions are often price-driven or availability-driven. The buyer has significant power because they can switch with zero friction.

MED

Supplier Power

Contract manufacturing is commoditised in Indian personal care. BSC doesn't own manufacturing, they outsource. This means margins are squeezed from both sides: retail demands discounts, manufacturers have limited flexibility.

HIGH

Threat of New Entrants

The D2C personal care space has almost no barriers to entry. A new men's grooming brand can launch on Amazon with contract-manufactured products, Instagram marketing, and ₹50L in capital. BSC's early-mover advantage has eroded as the space filled with competitors.

The Five Forces verdict: BSC faces high competitive pressure in every category it operates in, with low switching costs and weak category ownership. The mass pivot turned a defensible niche position (premium shaving) into an indefensible broad position (everything for men). They moved from a focused differentiation strategy to a stuck-in-the-middle position.

The One Thing They're Doing Wrong

SKU proliferation killed the brand's moat

Issue tree: why is brand recall weakening?

Why is BSC losing competitive differentiation?

Positioning dilution

50+ SKUs across 6 categories. No single category owned. "Men's grooming" is a shelf label, not a positioning.

Channel shift

D2C → retail/marketplace. Subscription model abandoned. No recurring revenue anchor.

Price compression

Mass retail demands lower price points. Premium margins erode. Competing on price against Gillette and Nivea.

The MECE breakdown is clear: BSC's differentiation problem has three non-overlapping root causes, positioning dilution, channel shift, and price compression, all of which stem from the same strategic decision to go mass. The primary driver (positioning dilution) is the one most within the team's control to fix.

The brand recall test: Ask 10 urban Indian men "what is Bombay Shaving Company?" In 2018, the answer would be "razors" or "shaving." In 2025, the answer is likely "men's grooming" or "that brand I've seen on Amazon." The shift from a specific association to a generic one is the clearest signal that the moat has eroded.

The Recommendation

Pick a lane: re-anchor around shaving or commit to the grooming platform

BSC has two viable paths. The current middle path, being everything for every man, is not one of them.

Path A: Re-anchor on shavingPath B: Commit to grooming platform
What it meansSunset underperforming SKUs (body wash, hair removal). Rebuild subscription model around razor + cream refills. Own "premium shaving" again.Accept the broad positioning. Build a loyalty/membership tier that bundles products. Compete on convenience + bundle economics.
The betA focused brand with loyal subscribers beats a broad brand with low repeat rates.Men's grooming is a platform play, whoever owns the first purchase owns the shelf.
The riskThe premium shaving market may be too small to justify ₹200Cr+ in funding.Competing against Gillette, Nivea, and Beardo simultaneously requires massive marketing spend.
Who winsDollar Shave Club model. Harry's model. Clear precedent in the US.Dr. Squatch model. Manscaped model. Requires scale.

My recommendation is Path A. The subscription razor model has proven economics globally. BSC has the brand equity to reclaim it. And it's the path that most directly rebuilds a retention loop, the one thing they lost in the mass pivot.

What I'd measure in 30 days

Unaided brand recall in "premium shaving" vs "men's grooming", surveyed across 500 urban Indian men aged 22–35.

If BSC still has stronger recall in "premium shaving" than in "men's grooming," the original positioning is recoverable. If recall is equally weak in both, the brand has been fully commoditised and the rebuild is harder. This single data point determines which strategic path is viable.
