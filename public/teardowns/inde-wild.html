inde wild: the acquisition machine that forgot to build a retention loop

ayurvedic beauty $12M ARR (2025) sephora uk and us 28 employees
brand snapshot

the "rhode of india", and what that comparison actually says

inde wild was founded in 2022 by diipa büller-khosla and her husband oleg. they took ayurvedic ingredients, wrapped them in gen z aesthetics, called it "ayurvedistry", and ran. three years in, they've hit $12m arr, landed in sephora uk and the us (first indian beauty brand to do so), and built a 3m+ following. all of which is genuinely impressive.

the champi hair oil is the engine. #1 prestige hair oil on nykaa, ahead of olaplex and l'oréal, reportedly selling a bottle a minute. the dewy lip treatment is the second hero. that's the full product story. two products, doing most of the work.

the "rhode of india" thing isn't wrong. both brands convert founder pull into product trial faster than almost anyone else. but rhode works because hailey bieber's addressable audience is effectively the whole internet. diipa's audience, while huge for india, is finite. that single fact changes the math.

growth diagnosis

the funnel works. except where it doesn't.

their north star is probably gmv. it shouldn't be.

based on how they talk about themselves in fundraise materials, they're almost certainly tracking monthly gmv or new customer acquisition rate. the whole $1.9m to $12m story is a top-line story. sephora expansion is a top-line move. everything points to a team optimising for growth velocity.

what they should be tracking: 90-day repurchase rate by sku. a beauty brand makes its margins on the second and third purchase, not the first. if champi oil buyers aren't coming back inside a single usage cycle (6 to 8 weeks for hair oil), no amount of new sephora shelves saves the underlying economics.

why acquisition is so strong

three things compound here: diipa's 3m+ personal following gives them free organic discovery; the packaging is genuinely beautiful, which makes unboxing content basically inevitable; and the "ayurvedistry" positioning sits in a category space nobody else owns. most d2c brands have one acquisition channel that's working. inde wild has three running at the same time. that's rare.

where the funnel breaks: a first-person data point

i tried it myself. bought the champi oil and the dewy lip treatment. the packaging is gorgeous, the products are good. i didn't buy either again. the math just didn't hold up for me. at ₹1,200+ for a hair oil, there are options at half the price that do most of what i need. the brand is in my head. it's not in my bathroom cabinet. that's the gap. pretty enough to try, not sticky enough to stay.

data analysis

the price problem: what champi oil is up against

when your hero product costs more per ml than almost everything in the category, you need one of two things. either a clearly better experience, or a reason the customer can't easily switch. inde wild has neither right now. the experience is good. it isn't twice as good as a ₹500 oil.

risk analysis

the one-product problem

you can't see their internal numbers, but you can infer a lot from public signals. nykaa bestseller positioning, the products they push in interviews, the items sephora chose to feature. all of it points the same way: most of inde wild's revenue comes from two products.

champi hair oil, est. 55 to 60% of revenue
dewy lip treatment, est. 15 to 20%
everything else, est. 20 to 25%

why this matters: someone will launch a comparable ayurvedic hair oil at 40% less, with packaging that looks just as nice. not a question of if. when. and on that day, the brand finds out how much of its growth was actually product loyalty versus aesthetic novelty. one product doing 55%+ of revenue isn't a foundation for a ₹1000cr brand. it's a single point of failure with great branding.

the one thing they're doing wrong

they've built an acquisition machine. that's a different thing from a brand.

look at what inde wild actually does. founder content, aesthetic launches, sephora shelves, every press feature. it's all designed to do one job: get a new person to buy for the first time. every channel, every campaign, every photo shoot, the same job.

what they don't have is anything that brings people back. no subscription. no "you're running low" prompt in the packaging or the inbox. no loyalty layer. no content that builds a daily or weekly habit (the kind kama ayurveda does well with its weekend rituals). the post-purchase experience is: buy → use → forget → see another instagram post → maybe buy. maybe.

this works at $12m arr. the pool of "people who follow indian beauty influencers and shop prestige beauty" is still large enough that you can keep growing on new customers alone. it doesn't work at ₹1000cr. by then the easy customers are gone, and every new one costs more to find. that's when you discover whether your earlier customers came back or not.

the back-of-envelope math: at a likely aov of ₹1,500 and an estimated blended cac of ₹300 to 500, each customer needs to come back 2 to 3 times for the unit economics to work. if their actual 90-day repurchase rate is sitting under 25%, they're spending more to acquire each customer than that customer will ever spend with them. the top-line keeps growing. the economics underneath may not.

the recommendation

stop selling a product. start selling a ritual.

champi (the actual word, the actual tradition) is a weekly hair oiling ritual. that's how my mother does it. that's how most indian families have done it for generations. inde wild named the product after the ritual. but they don't sell it as one. they sell it as a hair oil. that's the gap. the fix isn't a new sku or a discount. it's turning a product purchase into a recurring behaviour.

what i'd actually do:

1. a ritual card in the box. a printed card inside every champi oil package. "your 6-week champi ritual" with a checklist for each weekend. low cost, high signal. it tells the buyer this is a ritual, not a one-off. and it tells them when to expect to run out, before they actually do.

2. a refill prompt timed to the cycle. at week 4 of the 6-week cycle, send a whatsapp or sms: "your champi ritual is halfway through. lock in the next bottle at 15% off, delivered before you run out." the goal is to lock in the second purchase before the customer starts comparison shopping. that window is small. miss it and they're back on amazon looking at arata.

3. content that builds the habit, not the brand. weekly "champi sunday" instagram content. real customers doing the ritual, tagged and featured. the goal here isn't brand awareness, it's habit reinforcement. if a buyer sees other buyers doing this every sunday, they start doing it every sunday too. social proof beats every discount you could run.

what i'd measure in 30 days

if i were inside inde wild for a month, i'd track one thing.

90-day repurchase rate, broken down by sku and acquisition channel.

if champi oil sits above 30%, the problem is manageable, just push frequency. if it's below 20%, no sephora expansion fixes the underlying gap. and if the dewy lip treatment repurchase comes in higher than champi, that's a huge tell. one product has a real habit loop. the other is being bought for the aesthetic.
