Fae Beauty: The near-death pivot that turned hero-product focus into a growth engine

How a brand that was burning ₹2.5Cr/year on losses became profitable by doing less, and what the quick commerce channel shift reveals about modern activation.
Inclusive Cosmetics ₹22 Cr FY25 (projected) Shark Tank S4, ₹100Cr valuation 26 employees
Brand Snapshot

"Free and Equal", the brand that almost died trying to be everything

Founded in 2018 by Karishma Kewalramani (ex-management consultant, Berkeley grad), Fae Beauty was born from a clear insight: 74% of Indian women have brown to dark brown skin, yet most beauty products are formulated and marketed for fair skin. FAE (Free And Equal) set out to fix this with vegan, cruelty-free cosmetics designed for real Indian skin tones.

The mission was right. The execution nearly killed the company. From FY21 to FY23, Fae burned through capital, expanding its SKU range without a clear hero product, spending on marketing without unit economics discipline. Revenue crawled from ₹30 lakhs to ₹3.3 crore while losses peaked at ₹2.5 crore.

The pivot came in FY24: stop building new products, focus on one hero SKU (Lip Whip), and shift distribution to quick commerce. The results were dramatic, revenue doubled, losses shrank, and by October 2024 the company was profitable for the first time.

The inflection is FY24. Revenue doubles while losses shrink by 68%. The driver isn't a new product or a funding round, it's a subtraction. Fae stopped chasing SKU breadth and concentrated resources on one hero product with proven demand. This is the activation lesson: making one thing undeniably easy to buy beats making 20 things available.

Growth Diagnosis

Quick commerce as an activation channel, not just a distribution one

Fae's quick commerce pivot contributed 25% of revenue within 3 months of launch. That's not just a channel shift, it's an activation redesign. Here's why:

On Nykaa or the D2C website, a first-time buyer faces: browse 15+ SKUs → read descriptions → compare shades → add to cart → wait 3–5 days for delivery. Decision cost is high, time-to-value is long. Most visitors bounce.

On Blinkit or Zepto: see Lip Whip → add to cart → receive in 10 minutes. The activation friction is nearly zero. The product arrives before the impulse fades. Quick commerce collapsed the funnel from 5 steps to 2.

The One Thing They're Doing Wrong

One hero product can't carry a ₹100 crore brand

The Lip Whip focus saved Fae. It also created a new dependency. The Shark Tank valuation of ₹100 crore assumes continued 200%+ growth, but Lip Whip alone has a ceiling. The total addressable market for a single lip product SKU in India, even at Fae's price point, caps out well before ₹100 crore.

Fae needs a second hero product. Not a new category. Not 10 new SKUs. One product that the existing Lip Whip buyer naturally wants next. The mistake would be returning to the pre-pivot strategy of SKU proliferation. The right move is data-driven basket expansion: what do Lip Whip buyers already search for, already add to cart alongside it, already ask about in reviews?

The Shark Tank math: At a ₹100Cr valuation and ₹22Cr revenue, the implied revenue multiple is ~4.5x. For a beauty brand to justify that, investors expect 100%+ growth for 2–3 more years. A single hero SKU can't compound at that rate without a second product pulling its weight.

The Recommendation

Find the "second basket item" from existing purchase data

Don't guess the next hero product. Let the data tell you. The quick commerce platforms (Blinkit, Zepto) have basket-level data showing what Lip Whip buyers are already purchasing alongside it. Nykaa has search-after-purchase data. The D2C site has browse-after-purchase patterns.

The hypothesis: Lip Whip buyers are likely adding a lip-adjacent product (gloss, tinted balm) or a complementary step (foundation, concealer for brown skin tones). The product that already appears most frequently in the same basket or the same session is the candidate for hero product #2.

Build that one product. Give it the Lip Whip treatment: best-in-class for brown skin, hero positioning, quick commerce activation. Don't launch it as "new SKU." Launch it as "the second step in the Fae routine."

What I'd measure in 30 days

% of Lip Whip buyers who purchase a second FAE SKU within 60 days.

If below 10% → the product catalog isn't cross-selling and the second hero is urgent. If above 20% → identify which SKU is already winning and double down. The gap between these two numbers is the growth opportunity.
